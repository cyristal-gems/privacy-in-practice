# Sources and publication policy

Privacy in Practice explains source-backed privacy and cybersecurity records in plain language. Each record links to the legal text behind its description and shows when sources were checked.

## What we publish

We use enacted laws, regulations, agency publications and court decisions. When we use a published copy instead of the original host, the source label identifies that distinction. Commentary can help locate a source, but does not by itself prove that a bill became law or that a court entered an order.

Records with explicit unresolved factual gaps have been removed from this website project. The original working records and uncertainty notes are preserved in a separate archive outside the delivered project. No missing date, remedy, exception or legal status is replaced by a guess.

## How to read the links

Source lists expand beside the detail they support. PDF files are labeled and open separately. Where a large original volume is needed, a shorter relevant-page extract may be available alongside the full original. A file size is shown only when known.

## What the dates mean

Approval dates, effective dates and compliance deadlines may differ. If an effective date is calculated from a general legal rule, the entry says so. A source-review date means the source was checked on that date; it is not a guarantee against later changes.

## Coverage limits

Every state has published records, but that does not establish that every applicable law is included. A category with no published entry is not evidence that the state has no protection. The Research page provides a state selector, category-by-category evidence and the publication methodology.
